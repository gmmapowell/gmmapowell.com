@Now
date=2026-10-15


I don't know how much longer I can carry on.


I remember - back when I was a teenager, and frequently suicidal - thinking about how could it would be to have a switch on my alarm clock that wasn't "alarm at this time" or "don't bother waking me up" but "I don't want to wake up, ever".  But, as part of this thought experiment, I never actually did say "I don't want to wake up, ever", just "I don't want to have to deal with tomorrow".  I wanted to get through the bad bits - usually to the next weekend, sometimes longer.


And then, after I'd left school, I almost didn't think about that again for another thirty years, until a couple of years ago.  And then I said, "no, I really don't see how life gets any better from here on; I'm happy to go to sleep tonight and never wake up again".  And that's been fairly constant over the past couple of years.  Only now, it's got to the point where generally the first thing I think when I wake up is "oh, f***.  Another day to be got through, somehow".


So, what's wrong with me, then? Physically, I'm not disabled or anything. I have health issues and I fully expect to die (probably of a heart attack) in the next few years, but fairly obviously that doesn't worry me as much as it seems like an opportunity. 


No, the problem is that life is inherently pointless - and that fact is excruciatingly obvious to me.  In the absence of any inherent point, I need to make up some purpose on a daily basis. And that is becoming increasingly difficult. 


For the longest time, I viewed the creation of software as the "point" of my existence. But, over the past few years, both my ability to do that, and my ability to focus on that have degraded to the point that I can't really be bothered any more. 


And then the sheer costs of carrying on - getting up in the morning, feeding myself, the whole lot - is just too much hassle to be worth it. 


I spent the entire time between being 26 and being 52 feeling I was 26.  Then, for a few years, I felt my age. Now, even though I'm not yet 60, I feel like I'm 70-something.  My Dad died the day before his 64th birthday. He'd seemed fighting fit when I'd seen him earlier in the week. I can only hope to escape as easily. 


One day at a time... Cognitive load
