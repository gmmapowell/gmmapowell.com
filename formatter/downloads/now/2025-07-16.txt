@Now
date=2025-07-16


I have wanted to add a $/now$ section to my website for a while now.


The problem I have run into is knowing what to say that doesn't require a detailed explanation of everything that has gone before.  This is a problem I have a lot - the "book" I'm currently writing, for example, started off being fairly short before I realised it needed an introductory preface, which in turn needed more introduction until eventually I'd written almost 100,000 words before I'd reached my original starting point.


On this occasion, I decided the obvious thing to do was to "backfill" my $/now$ entries as if I had written them in the past, although each one is clearly labelled as being written now.


I'm not entirely sure where the low point in my life was, but it was somewhere around the turn of the year.  All the things I usually focus on - software, writing, reading - to distract me from the passage of life had "stopped working".  I couldn't focus, I couldn't care, I couldn't find anything I was interested in.  I still had a "forever" list of things that, theoretically, I wanted to get done, but I didn't actually want to settle down and _do_ any of them.  And all of the ones I would consider "most important" or "most valuable" had high barriers to re-entry: there was a significant cost to starting to code them again.


What finally got me moving?  Back in November last year, I was talking to someone about blockchains, and an idea that had been buzzing around in my head for about a decade finally came together with its solution while I was having a burger in Five Guys.  The intensity of the challenge started to drag me out of the existential funk I had been in since my daughter had cut us off back in March.


But momentum is a funny thing.  More bad things happened with my daughter in January, and dented my confidence and my desire to keep working on a blockchain product.  Instead, out of frutration, I started working on another side project: trying to get an NFC card reader working with an Android phone.  Incredibly, a thing I would normally find incredibly frustrating - absolutely nothing working for a couple of days while I scrabbled around for answers - actually served to motivate me.  Almost as if I could manage to concentrate all my rage and frustration onto solving this problem.  As if solving this problem would solve the bigger problems in my life.


Once I threw that monkey off my back, I turned back to ChainLedger and made that at least "code complete": setting out, my main goal was to demonstrate techniques for testing distributed systems and I still haven't done that.  But now I was more excited about solving a "real work" problem: deploying infrastructure to the cloud.  This had come up (again) in the context of building ChainLedger: as well as demonstrating how the system could be tested, I wanted to show how it worked "at scale, in the real world" and that involved deploying complex API Gateway and Lambda elements in the cloud.  But this time, again, seemingly magically, the solution I had been seeking presented itself to me.  A couple of quick experiments with Go and plugins later, I was off and running.  Slowly, oh so slowly, I dragged myself out of the pit and got back into writing serious software, writing my book, writing website content, fixing up my website.


I feel I'm back.


For the curious, my personal life continues to suck.
