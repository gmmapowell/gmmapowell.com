title=What's in a Title?
saveAs=life/title


For those who don't know, Bruce Springsteen is my hero(*j. 


Also, for those who don't know, his current tour started in Manchester in May 2025 (I went to the third show) and is billed as the "Land of Hope and Dreams tour". Just to be sure we knew this, he announced it in a two minute speech before he played a note of music. In that time, he told us that he was here to garner opposition to the "corrupt and incompetent regime" which had taken over America.


Once he had finished saying all of that, he launched into "No Surrender".


As I listen back to the show over and over (well, maybe not that much, but every time I'm at the gym), I'm struck by the fact that he chose the _optimistic_ title "Land of Hopes and Dreams tour" rather than the more combative "No Surrender tour".


And obviously I start wondering why.


Don't get me wrong.  Song to song, Land of Hope and Dreams is by far the better song.  Indeed, the story goes that No Surrender was only added to the Born in the USA album at the last moment.  As a song, it's simply not that good.


But take a song out on tour, and, much like Lewis Carroll's Humpty Dumpty, Bruce pays them a lot more to mean what he wants them to mean.  On the Born in the USA tour forty-plus years ago, he slowed it right down and played it acoustic, turning it from a rocker into a song that acknowledged frailty and the loss of youth and innocence.  For years now, it has been an "encore song" that has been there to flick the switch to "party mode".  So just by putting it at the start of the show, Bruce made a statement.


If you listen to the words of the song, it's clear that in 1983 Bruce recognized "No Surrender" as a teenage reaction to a difficult and trying world.  As a teenager, Bruce found the way the world worked incomprehensible and was determined to stick to his dreams _no matter what_.  By the time he wrote the song, he had realised his dreams and looked back on simpler times, recognising that even when you _say_ "no retreat, no surrender", you will end up retreating from something, in some way, at some point.


So by putting it where he put it, with the spoken introduction on this tour, he was making it do a _lot_ of work that it isn't really capable of lifting.  But nevertheless, somehow, he pulls it off.  Somehow, it becomes a song of protest against all the injustices of the Trump regime.


Land of Hope and Dreams is _not_ that.  It is a positive, optimistic song about equality, opportunity and the idea that there is hope for us all, brought by some mythical "train" - and all _we_ need to do is "get on board".  We don't even, apparently, need a ticket.  When I first heard it (Charlotte, 4/21/00), I could only make out half the words, but even so I was struck by how clearly this sent a message about where Bruce was at that time.


OK, to my point.


It is very telling (to me) that in this time and place, in spite of (or because of) everything that is going on in America, Bruce chooses to focus on the positive, on what _could be_ rather than resistance to what is.  It is, in my mind, a statement about who he is, how he sees things and how he prioritizes hope over despair.


I do not.  To me, I see what is clearly in front of me and focus on that.  Yes, I can see into the future, and I can see how things _could_ be better, and yes, I suppose I use that as some sort of guiding light, but first and foremost, I feel the need to deal with today's problems.  Without fear or favour.


"No Retreat, baby, No Surrender"


(*) I want to be clear that Bruce is my hero, he is not my "idol".  I don't agree with him on everything (including the title of this tour) and I don't slavishly defend things I don't actually agree with, or think that all his songs are fantastic.  But I _do_ think that he looks at the world clearly and critically and sees to the bottom of a lot of issues that other people just ignore.
