@Article
title=Love is not All You Need
saveAs=life/love-need


The Beatles released a song called "All you Need is Love".  From the first time I'd heard it, this seemed a bizarre notion to me.  But even if you accept the apologists' notion that what they are saying is not literally that love is _all_ you need, but merely that love is undervalued, I still can't get on board.


The thing is, that even if what you are saying is that human relationships are worth more than all of the success, money and fame on offer, love is simply not enough to scale that mountain.  In any relationship, it helps enormously if there is love on one side or the other.  It's possible to argue that love on both sides is enough to create the "space" to have a working relationship.  But there are still so many other factors.


This may just be a personal bias, but the first one I would always list is _alignment_.  No matter how much two people might love each other, if they don't want to go in the same direction, something needs to give: if one person wants to live in Germany while the other wants to live in Brazil (or, indeed, if they already do), then one of them has to give up their desired lifestyle or the two of them cannot reasonably be together.


You may say "that's ridiculous" but remember that I am a key proponent of exaggeration to make a point and _ad absurdum_ arguments.  It's easy to see the problem in this case, but many other things are more subtle but no less injurious.  Consider the trade-offs involved in having children: it is almost always to the benefit of the children to stay in one place and to be surrounded by extended family; pursuing a career generally implies being free to change jobs and travel around the country if not the world; to say nothing of the conflicts that arise if one or other partner suffers from wanderlust.


Yes, you say, but that just requires _compromise_.  Almost, I think, my point exactly.  Love is not all you need.  You need a range of skills including compromise and empathy.  You need the ability to enter into, and respect, a dialogue.  You need the ability to hear what someone else has to say with equanimity.  And you both (or all) need the ability to agree on a best way forward that meets the most needs.


Love may give you the incentive, and the willingness, to do that.  But it can't make it happen.
