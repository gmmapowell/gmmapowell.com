title=Say what you Mean!
saveAs=life/say-mean


It annoys me no end when people say one thing and mean something completely different.


At the moment, this is inspired more than anything by the current trend of servers in coffee shops to greet you with "are you OK?" or "how are you doing?".  I have two choices: one is to answer this question, and then stand there for some long time before they ask the question they really want to ask; or I can answer a different question, which is something like "what would you like to order?".


It is especially annoying when the moment I start telling them what I would like to order, they interrupt to ask some process question, such as "will you be staying in or taking away?"  If that is the first question you want the answer to, why don't you ask me that the moment I walk in, rather than asking me a nonsense question?


Yes, I do realise that this is much more a statement about me than it is about them, although I would argue that it is mainly a statement about what we as a society have normalised.  When I first went to the US, I was staggered how interested everybody was in my physical and mental health, and how important the quality of my life was to them.  It was only after a significant amount of time there (we are talking some number of months) that I realised the questions were formal rather than genuine: the question "How are you doing?" went with the answer "Fine", and "Have a nice day" went with "And you".  It was, if you like, syntactic sugar around the bulk of the conversation.


Why did this surprise me so much?  Because in those days, this _did not happen_ in England.  Inasmuch as anybody ever asked me how I was doing, it was because they did, actually, care.  If somebody just wanted to greet me, they would say "Hello" or at least "Good morning" (which, while most people don't think about it, at least just a greeting and not a question).


The real problem with _asking a question_ as a greeting is that it solicits a response.  And if you don't know that it isn't actually a question but just a greeting, you might not give the _correct_ answer, which is "fine".  And thus as you start to tell them what you're going through today, you realise they are not only not interested, they are literally confused: why are you telling them this?  Their experience is just the same as it would be if I said "that will be two pounds fifty, please" and you started telling me your life story.  Where is this going?  Why are you saying this?


I have learnt over the years how to navigate this minefield, and it has stood me in good stead as this has increasingly become the trend in England (probably influenced in large part by a whole generation growing up watching _Friends_).  But, I'm going to claim that my brain is wired differently: even as I know the answer is "fine" or whatever, my brain is generating the correct answer and won't stop.  And it gives me cognitive dissonance every time I hear something that isn't what it means.


I know, this ship has sailed and I just need to get over it.
