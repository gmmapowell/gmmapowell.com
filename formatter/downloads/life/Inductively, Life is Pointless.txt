@Article
title=Inductively, Life is Pointless
saveAs=life/induction


Life is pointless.  I have known this for a long time.


When I say "a long time", I mean since before I first used a computer, when I was trying to figure out why I hated being alive so much and trying to understand if that was ever going to change.  So long ago that (almost) everything that I am going to say in this article must _logically_ have been something I discovered after the event because I simply _did not know_ about induction when I was 12.


In some sense, at that point I was looking for a _telos_.  I didn't know about that, and, while I was learning Latin at school, I have never, really, learnt any Greek.  I didn't know anything about Greek philosophy until I was about 20, and I didn't know about Aristotle and _telos_ until I was around 40.  So I can't claim that I was _literally_ doing that.  But I was.


What is the point of life?  In other words, for what reason are we put here?  Assuming that we are _put_ here in some sense (growing up, everybody around me believed this or claimed they did: it was a recurring message at home, at church, in school).  I felt that I could use reasoning (I had not encountered formal logic at the time) to prove "from first principles" (something I had just come into contact with in first year maths - Euclidean geometry) what I should be doing and how I should be tackling my life if I knew what the end point was.


And I ended up with what I now recognize to be a two-directional inductive proof that life is inherently pointless.


Starting from where I was, at age 12, I asked myself what the "point" of going to school was.  To get good "O" and "A" level results.  OK, bit of a waste of 7 years of life and if every minute of it wasn't a living hell, I could tolerate that (let's leave to one side the ego-maniacal belief that I could have passed those exams with a few books and a few past papers in about two years left to my own devices).  OK, so what's the point of getting good exam results?  To go to University.  To get a "good" degree.  So that you can get a job.  So that you can get married.  So that you can have children.


OK.  And, uh, then?  Doesn't that just put those children back in the same spot I'm in _right now_?  OK, so I have just independently discovered induction.


At the same time, the other reason for having a job is to earn money.  So that you can buy things.  And stay alive.  But if I weren't alive, I wouldn't need things.  I wouldn't need money.  It's a very circular argument.  So I need to look outside myself.


One "reason" for being alive is to be altruistic.  To help other people.  It's hard to say whether, when you are growing up, the world is just starting to need "sorting out" or whether it is only as you grow older that _the fact_ that it needs sorting out descends on your consciousness.  And you start to realize just how seriously messed up everything is.  "I could sort that out".  But wait, why?


It is not unreasonable to say that "that is my place in life", but it surely cannot be the _point_ of life in general.  If it is _the point_ of life to sort life out for other people, then that means that those people must have some point to their lives.  So I could just jump tracks and have the same point that they have to their life.  Except, wait: they too are just trying to scrabble enough together to make a living to keep themselves alive and have children so that ...  OK, yeah, that's induction again.


You could say that the point of life is to look after the environment, except that the environment would do a lot better without us.  No doubt about it, if we all wanted to save the whale, the best thing we could do would be to end human life on earth.  That's the biggest threat to the whale.  Or dolphins.  Or beetles.  And the obvious contribution I could make would be to end _my_ life.  And I was trying to find an argument _against_ doing that.


Life, in short, is pointless.


I find it very hard to explain this to people (I hesitate to say "convince people of this", because I'm not actually trying to do that; what they choose to believe is up to them, but it's almost impossible to understand who I am or why I do any of the things that I do if you believe that life is inherently pointful).  They tend to counter with observations about what they find nice, or useful or good about being alive without seeming to realize that this has nothing to do with what the _point_ of life is.


As a comparison, the _point_ of a car is to get you around.  A car that doesn't go anywhere becomes, in some sense, pointless.  To remark that one of the benefits of life is that you get to smell roses is a bit like saying but at least a broken-down car "has a nice paint job".  OK, but that doesn't make it _a car_.


So I want to be _very_ clear about what I am and am not saying here.  I _am_ saying that there is no inherent point to life, and that "when you arrive" there is no place you are trying to get to.  I am _not_ saying that the only reasonable thing to do is to end your life the moment it starts.


For me, the big issue is that I always go back to first principles whenever I am trying to solve a problem. Those who know me professionally will know that my instinct on running into a software problem is to ask the question, "well, what does the user want?".  It's the same thing here: "what does life want of me?"  And the answer is nothing.  So when I come up against a sticky situation, it's left up to me.


I am fine with people telling me about the great things in life.  And I'm happy that they are happy with life.  And I understand that there is some sense in which I should be grateful that I'm alive to enjoy it all.  I understand them when they say "but you enjoy 'playing' on your computer, don't you?"  But that doesn't address the question of where you go when life _doesn't_ seem worth the hassle, because, after all, what's the point?


But it is very frustrating to have people just completely fail to understand the "point" I am trying to make.  The point is not about whether or not you are happy; or about whether or not there are things in life that are enjoyable, or worth doing; not even about whether the good outweighs the bad.  The point is just the utter pointlessness and futility of it all.  That, at the end of the day, whenever and however that may come, you may or may not have achieved any of the things that you set out to achieve.  _But none of it matters._  Almost undoubtedly, people will miss you.  And that is about it.


A possible analogy is consider turning up for a meeting that has been cancelled.  When I turn up, I'm told that the meeting I was supposed to attend isn't going to happen (say, because one of the other participants can't make it), but, perhaps, since you're here, you could help us with this issue that's come up?  Sure, they benefit from my being there, and my trip wasn't wasted, but that wasn't _the point_ of going, which was to have a meeting.  While I'm here on this planet, I might as well make "the most" of it, _but there is no inherent point to me being here_.


It's also frustrating to have people hear what I have to say and decide that I am some kind of "suicide risk" (I am still waiting for someone to tell me what the "risk" here consists of).  As if, as I am recounting something that I learnt 45 years ago, I am just discovering it at the moment they are hearing it and am just about to kill myself in front of them.  As if, in saying this, they don't realize that I have 45 years of coping strategies under my belt at the moment.  That, even if I do decide the time is "now", I'm not going to fling myself off a bridge in mid-sentence.


I feel I am always on thin ice; that the "truth" is just underneath the surface, waiting to gobble me up at any time.  As long as I stay focused on getting this program to work; or reading this book and understanding it; enjoying this holiday; helping my children to understand this; getting this meal ready; writing this content for my website; I am fine.  That becomes my all-encompassing "purpose" in life, and I can answer every question from that frame of reference (and yes, I can context switch between these easily).  But rip that away, and ask me why _any of it_ is important, and you will probably reduce me to a blubbering lump.  It's not.  There's no point to any of it and I might as well just give up now.


Except giving up is hard.  And, in the moment, any number of things act to keep you alive.  I really do feel that all the way back at school, when I was 12, it was only the "irrational" belief that things would "get better" that kept me alive.  That and how hard it literally is to "give up".  As I have grown older, I really feel that the connections I have made in life made keeping going the more sensible option.  For 20 years, I knew that my children literally depended on me to look after them.  I've been aware of how painful it would be for my wife to be left on her own.  And so on.


I automatically search for the purpose in things.  I've come to the conclusion that while I am undoubtedly not alone in this, this is in fact an incredibly rare trait (one in ten?  one in a hundred?).  That most people gloss over that and just look at the outward surface of things.  I am here, and I am doing this: therefore I must be here to do this.  I was in my 20s and engaged before I first came across Candide; but I can't help feeling that, while a caricature, Pangloss stands in for many of the people I know: this is the best of all possible worlds.


So, am I suicidal? Yes and no at the same time, and sometimes one more than the other.  I think that while my life may not have an inherent, external _purpose_, it is true that my life has meaning through the connections that I have made in my life.  My wife and son would both miss me if I were not here.  Some of my friends would feel that their lives were a little poorer without me in them.  Those things are in the accounting "credit" column.  So are the things that I enjoy doing.  They are weighted against the bad things in my life.  For now, the tally keeps coming up that my life is adding something to the world.  But when that turns, I do feel it should be my choice as to when to call it quits.
