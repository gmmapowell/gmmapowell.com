title=Truth and Falsehood

To tell the truth, or to tell the convenient lie?


OK, so that's _not_ what Hamlet said, although you could easily argue that it is the subtext of the play.  Everyone knows what's going on, and, in some quiet corners, people are talking about it.  But nobody comes out and says it.


Why not?


I'm sure there are many reasons, but as I sit here today, having not seen or spoken to my grandchildren for over a year because the lies that my daughter has spread about my wife and I have just been completely accepted and, in cases, not even _challenged_ by those hearing them (to the point that somebody I thought was my friend and knew us better than that told me I was "in denial" about our "unseen" mental health issues), I think an important one is just a plain lack of courage.


For all of us, every situation is a trade-off.  We can take the "moral high ground" with its associated costs, or we can take the easy path.  And when someone makes it plain that the taking the high road will have high costs, then we feel all the more inclined to take the easy road - the road that is easy "for us".


But what about everybody else?


Regular readers - and those who know me personally - will know that I see everything in terms of "a system".  If the system is functioning healthily, the individual actions are neither here nor there.  But when someone takes actions which significantly degrade the overall health of the system, I feel a moral imperative to act.


Failure to do so leads to &sp
&link 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_hazard' '"moral hazard"'
.  Apparently, wikipedia thinks this is *only* about economics, but to me its exactly the same in real life:  anyone who feels empowered to lie today for their own self-interest, and _then goes unchallenged_ by others in the system will, of course, be encouraged to do the same over and over - with the lies becoming more exaggerated, and more harmful as time progresses.


Just because you are not on the receiving end of this behaviour _today_ does not mean that you will not be _tomorrow_.  And if you take the attitude that it is perfectly acceptable to ignore this behaviour if it does not directly affect you, but only other actors in the system, then you are implicitly saying that when you _are_ on the receiving end, it is perfectably acceptable for everyone else to ignore your plight.


I don't agree with most of what &sp
&link 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant' 'Immanuel Kant'
&sp had to say, mainly because it lacked any sense of nuance (especially his idea that telling the truth was privileged above all other concerns - where obviously I would have the health and functioning of the overall system in that place), but I do agree that you should always act in a way that is consistent with how you would like the world to be.


If you don't, why should anyone else?
