title=Running To or From?
saveAs=life/running


I feel that I have spent most of my life "running".


It's one of those "there are two sorts of people in the world" things (or, if you prefer formal logic, there is a relation $R$ which divides the universal set of people into two): those who are continually running, and those who are happy to be where they find themselves.


Of late, though, I've noticed a difference in the way this manifests in my life.  In my youth (say, up until about 40), I was keen to achieve this or that: my focus was intense and it amazes me now the things I managed to get done while generally only working the prescribed 40 hours a week (it helped that 16 of them were from home!).


But lately, I tend to flit a lot more between projects.  I think there is a level at which I am still just as excited and energized about trying to do this or that, but I don't have the same dedication I used to have to sit down and make it happen for 8 hours at a stretch, five days a week.  It's funny how much I can actually get done these days at the rate of two or three hours a day, four days a week (yes, folks, the 10-15 hour workweek is productive, especially if you spend one day a week outside thinking), but the real problem is that it feels very dissipated across so many different projects.


Yes, yes, that's because I am interested in dozens of things.  And that's a good excuse.  All the time I want to be working on this, or doing a bit on that.  But that raises the question I came here today to ask: am I running _towards_ what I want to do, or _away_ from those things that I don't?


I have "always" been incredibly disciplined (OK, this is a statement I am only prepared to back up until about 2021).  Throughout my career, there have been tasks that I have been excited about, and there have been tasks that I have dreaded.  But regardless of how I _feel_ about a task, I have always set to, knowing that until it is done, it will be there, waiting for me to come back to it and apply myself.  And leaching away at my spirit until I face up to it.


I feel that leaching now, as I write this.  I am still drawn to things that I want to do, and I still have the discipline to do those things that I don't want to do, but, in my own life at least, I find it perfectly easy and acceptable to work for a couple of hours, then stop and do other things, frittering the day away.  If I'm particularly energized by a task, I'll keep at it, but when it starts to go uphill, I'll stop.  It's very instructive to see the pattern of my days: some I'll work six or eight hours; others I'll struggle to make one.  Fortunately, I still tend to work at the level of a "task", so I generally won't stop until I have completed a whole task.


And so I find myself flitting between tasks.  In the morning, I'll generally do software.  After lunch, I'll go out to a coffee shop and do something more creative (like writing a book, or this kind of content).  Maybe in the evening I'll do something that appeals to me.


But the big question remains: am I running towards the things I love doing, or am I running away from things I'm scared of?


Why does this matter?  Because if I am running towards something, I am picking something up because I'm energized about it.  If I'm running away from something, I'm dropping something that feels too hard.  It's a core part of my belief system that nothing is too hard for me (at least, if it's something that I've considered taking on).  So that says I am losing my discipline.


And that's a scary thing if you're me.
