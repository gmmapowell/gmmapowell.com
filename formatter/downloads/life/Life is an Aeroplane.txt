title=Life is an Aeroplane
saveAs=life/aeroplane


They tell me there comes a point in life when you become aware of your own mortality.  I think, for me, that was when I was 12.


But now that I am at the point where I think you are supposed to worry about the limited amount of time you have left, I feel more a sense of completion than a sense of panic.  The closest analogy I can draw is to a transatlantic flight.


When you take off, there is excitement: you see the ground whizzing by below you and you are climbing steadily.  There is a rough patch as you go through turbulent air over Ireland.  After that, there are the distractions of meal services and duty free until the long, boring section of the flight takes hold.  And then, after a fabulous afternoon tea, you settle in to the steady descent and the flight is, at long last, over.


My life has definitely followed this pattern: until I was 11, I enjoyed life and revelled in the excitement of what came up every day.  What followed was a brutal seven years of being at secondary school, where I think I can honestly say that every day was a trial getting out of bed.  As always, I want to be clear that the problem was the other children in the school, not the lessons themselves which could be interesting and even stimulating.


The following twenty years were full of diversions and distractions as I learned my trade and formed by own family.  As much as possible, I lived the life I wanted to live, choosing the path that I thought I wanted to follow.  Amazingly, for almost all of that time I felt I was 22.  It was always a shock to fill out a form and write "age 46" or whatever on it.  I did not feel that way.


After a boring 15 years, I had a lovely "afternoon tea" of my life (sometimes literally; thank you, S.!) spending about two years with my lovely grandchildren K. and S.; K. in particular brought joy with her wherever she went.


And now the captain has announced that we have begun our descent prior to landing on the far side of the Atlantic.  Bring on the landing, I say.  Whenever it comes is fine by me.  I have achieved all of the goals I set for myself when I was younger (to be an amazing software engineer; to write a book; to live in America) and done many other things I never considered (bringing up a family; living in Costa Rica).  I'm sure I will do many other things during this final phase of my life, but however exciting they may be, they will just be things to pass the time while I am, as it were, strapped into my seat with my seatbelt fastened and all big electronics put away.  I do not have a "bucket list", what would be the point?


I have always said that if I had an epitaph I would want it to be "Saith the Preacher" in a nod to those who know the quote from &sp
&link 'https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes%201%3A2-2&version=ERV' 'Ecclesiastes'
: "Vanity of vanities, saith the preacher, vanity of vanities: all is vanity".  But at this precise moment, this from Macbeth feels a better summary of my life:


$$
Life's but a walking shadow, a poor player,
That struts and frets his hour upon the stage,
And then is heard no more. It is a tale
Told by an idiot, full of sound and fury,
Signifying nothing.
$$


Of course, I have lived my life since age 12 _knowing_ that it signified nothing.  Including the two years I spent studying Macbeth for O level.
