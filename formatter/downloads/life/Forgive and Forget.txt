title=Forgive and Forget
saveAs=life/forgive


"Forgive and Forget" is common advice in the English-speaking world.  Up to a point, I think it is good advice.  The point is that while doing these things is positive, it's not enough.


IMHO, forgiveness is often misunderstood as being "for the benefit of the other person", but in fact the main beneficiary of forgiving someone is the person doing the forgiving.  Why?  Because for all the time you _don't_ forgive, the thought of the injury you suffered keeps eating at you.  Not only do you suffer from the injury itself, but you suffer from the cancer of _feeling_ injured and the injustice of it all.  You need to flush that from your system, and that process is what we call _forgiveness_.  It matters not at all if the other person knows that you have forgiven them.


Forgetting is harder, but again, the main beneficiary of flushing the memory of what happened is the injured party - until you do, it will keep assaulting you from the recesses of your mind.


"But," I hear you say.  "In that case..."


Exactly.  My point exactly.  This is positive, but it's not enough.


What you need to be sure of is that whatever bad thing happened to you _is not going to happen again_, to you or to anybody else.  Forgiveness does not get you there.  Forgetfulness does not get you there.  You need another step.  I think there are two formulations of this:


* Forgive, forget _and do not repeat_; or
* Forgive, forget _and change the system_.


The first is the simpler: it's important to realize that whatever happens, you do not want to repeat that behaviour.  It's also the more inclusive: it allows for any solution to the problem of repetition, the most obvious of which is to "eliminate" the offender, whether by making them redundant in a work setting, distancing yourself from them in a social setting, or incarcerating or executing them in a criminal setting.


The second recognizes that most of the time, it's possible to avoid a repetition of an injury by making sure that the situation does not arise again.  How this is achieved depends to a great extent on what the system is and what the nature of the injury is.


It generally isn't possible to go back in time and make things "right", but if you change the system so that you can be confident that the same thing will never happen again, it is a lot easier forgiving, forgetting and continuing to have a relationship with "the offender".


Including when you are "the offender".
