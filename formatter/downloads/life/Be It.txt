title=Be It, Do It, Say It
saveAs=life/be-it


The idea of "alignment" is very important to me.


In the course of my life, I've found that this is rare.  Most people are happy to say "well, yes, that is what I tell other people, but I don't do it myself".  Wow.  Why not?


Immanuel Kant observed that you should always behave in a way which, if everybody behaved that way, you would not criticize other people for their actions.  Nothing is ever this simple of course, and no doubt elsewhere on this website I've pointed out how important it is to be nuanced in your understanding of situations.


But there is a very simple principle which I try to adhere to and which seems to me covers most of the cases.  And this is "be it - do it - say it".


It all starts with _being_.  Who do you want to be?  What do you want other people to think of you?  What do you want to think of yourself?  Is that what you are?  Again, I'm not claiming this is easy, but if you are _not_ the person you believe you want to be, you are out of alignment.  You should either change your belief about what you should be, or you should change what you are.


If you feel that you want, for example, to be more disciplined, I have to ask: what is stopping you?  Why can you not change today to be a more disciplined person?  If, when you think about it, you realize you don't actually want to change that way, then give up the self-destructive belief that you do.


This follows through into _actions_.  If you want to _be_ more disciplined, then you have to act in a more disciplined fashion.  You say you want to go to the gym twice a week.  What is _actually_ stopping you?  Again, it is fine to _not_ want to go to the gym (at all, never mind twice a week) but when you believe that you _do_ want to go, and then _don't_ go, you are out of alignment.  Why?


And then comes the third thing, which is what you say.  If you are in alignment, you will believe something, you will act it out in your daily life, and then you will tell other people that that is what you do.


If you are out of alignment, you will say something that is either not aligned with who you are or what you do.  You may say "I go to the gym twice a week" when you don't, or you may say "I would like to go to the gym twice a week but ..." when what comes after the "but" is something other than "I don't really want to".


It is my belief that living out of alignment is harmful and (if you are doing it), you need to stop it.  The easiest fix is to what you _say_.  Be honest (with yourself as much as others) and say how you really feel: "I would like to be fitter, but I recognize that the only way I can reasonably make that happen is by going to the gym, and I would rather go to McDonald's".  It doesn't sound as good, does it?  But if it's honest...


It's harder to change your actions.  It requires time, discipline, dedication and effort.  But it can be done.  It's obvious "how": take the things that you say, and bend your actions into alignment with them, _whatever it takes_.  If it really is too hard, think about why, and then _say_ that instead of whatever it is that you think you believe.


Changing who you are is almost impossible and the only real advice I can give you is "to fake it until you make it": change your actions and keep going with those until that applies so much pressure to who you are that it changes you.  The simpler (and possibly better) approach is to accept who you are, and to align your speech and actions with that.
