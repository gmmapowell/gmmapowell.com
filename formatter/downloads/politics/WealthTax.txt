Title=Wealth Tax 
SaveAs=politics/wealthtax


I think there is a general consensus that a universal, global wealth tax would be a good idea. The problem is that in too many countries, the government is in hock to the richest individuals, who can buy representatives for much less money than they would have to pay under a wealth tax (given that even at a modest 1%, each billionaire would be paying at least $10m).


So, can individual countries go it alone? Well, yes, but there are a couple of additional challenges that would not otherwise arise. 


Firstly, it is possible for individuals to "hide" their assets in countries or jurisdictions that do not report these assets to the country implementing the tax; and, secondly, it is possible for individuals to flee the jurisdiction implementing the tax, thus avoiding it altogether. 


While no solution can be perfect, I believe that it is possible to implement a tax effectively which overcomes these challenges. 


First off, an implementing country can require all eligible persons to declare their worldwide wealth. Failure to include some assets (for example, those hidden in tax shelters) could result in the confiscation of those, or equivalent assets up to the total sum being concealed. If you don't declare it, you clearly don't care about it or need it, so you can afford to do without it. Obviously, before such draconian measures are put into place, the government should ensure that it is genuine tax evasion and not simply an oversight. Furthermore, if the amount of money that can be confiscated is insufficient to meet the amount that has been hidden, the individual may be sent to prison for an indefinite amount of time until the amount is paid.
