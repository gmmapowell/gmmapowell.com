title=Immigration is a Category Error
saveAs=politics-musings/immigration


I was boarding a bus the other day when somebody was ranting about the state of the world in general, the NHS in particular and blaming it on immigrants.  The idiocy and ignorance were breathtaking.


I generally don't get into arguments with "people like that" in situations like that because it just isn't worth it to me.  The most likely outcome is that I will be even more frustrated.  So I've come here to vent, instead, where I can do so in the privacy of my own mind and at least get my story straight before anyone disagrees.


I'm not going to argue that immigrants do more harm than good, or that we need them, or discuss in detail how we would need to change as a society to cope without them.  I'm not going to discuss birth rates and population demographics although I'm &sp
&link 'https://www.theguardian.com/world/ng-interactive/2025/feb/18/europes-population-crisis-see-how-your-country-compares-visualised' 'aware of articles'
&sp out there that do discuss all these things.


I'm going to say that talking about immigration and whether you support it or oppose it is simply a &sp
&link 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_mistake' 'category error'
.  Because I always find it easier to work by analogy and then come back to where I started to make my point, I'm going to say, it's like being opposed to cars.


If you were Nigel Farage and went on TV saying you were opposed to cars, everybody would look at you as if you were mental (well, fair enough, but even if you weren't Nigel Farage).  What does it even mean?  Are you opposed to the existence of cars? Of traffic?  Of people moving around the country?  Of mobility in general?  Or just specific aspects: pollution, accidents, road noise, villages being spoilt, bypasses being built, the waste that is car parks?


You probably could argue that everything about cars is bad, and that therefore cars _themselves_ are bad.  Personally, I have never heard this argument, and I think most people think that *their* car is fine, but that it would be better if nobody else had one.  Everybody agrees that the environmental damage they do, and the impact on villages and life is general is bad, but that on the whole, "what's the alternative"?


I *almost* think that could be a rational attitude to immigration, except for one thing.


What even _is_ immigration?  It is people coming "from another country".  Can you hold up a minute there, so like people coming from Cardiff in Wales to London?  Uh, no, that doesn't count because it's apparently "the same country" (is it?  or isn't it?).


OK, so more like people from Manchester going and living in Fuengirola, then?  Or people from Birmingham moving to Sydney, Australia?  That's what you mean.  No, apparently those are fine, too.


I'm going to nail my colours to the mast and say that, for me, countries are stupid and arbitrary demarcations between people.  Artificial barriers.  As a systems thinker, I see the world as a whole as one big system, and each "country" within it as part of that system.  Why build walls between them?  It makes the system less efficient.  As Trump embarks on his trade war, I think we will see that over the next few months and years.


So why the barriers, then?  _Cui bono_ (who benefits)?  Well, if you see that some countries are "richer" than others (in whatever way), then obviously people want to be in those richer countries.  And thus people in those countries want to keep other people out.


OK, so opposition to immigation _qua_ immigration is then just an elitist thing.  OK.
