In &sp
&link './why-estimate' 'another article'
&sp I went into great detail about how it might be possible to obtain accurate estimates if a client wants them, and how incredibly difficult and expensive it is.  Reading that article back today (in 2025; the article was written in 2008), I almost wonder if I was being satirical.  However, while I may have been _frustrated_, I was endeavouring to be serious.  And, in particular, I was keen to make two _huge_ points:


* It's really difficult to estimate "something" without knowing exactly what that "something" is;
* The number of dimensions (knowable and unknowable) is so large that even obtaining a handle on them is almost impossible.


In this article, I want to come at the whole question of estimation from a different (possibly more truculent) perspective: tell me why you want this estimate and what you're going to do with it and I'll give you some, limited, information.


There are three main things that developers think clients want estimates for:


* To hold against them later when the client is upset with what has been delivered;
* To fill in pretty charts and diagrams for them to show to investors;
* To plan out marketing and sales campaigns.


While I think all of these things happen, I think only the last one is genuinely valid.  But having worked closely with clients over the years and _asked_ them why they want an estimate, most commonly it is quite simple: _to make decisions_.  Given any feature $X$, the client knows that they "want" it, but they also want features $Y$ and $Z$ (and, usually, 2000 others).  But they know enough to know they are resource-limited: so which of these stories should they ask for?


They probably haven't calculated the dollar value of any of these features, but they probably have an instinct about the relative _value_ of the three.  And what they want from developers is a similar instinct about the relative _cost_ of the three.  And, in line with many developers before me, as long as we can agree on what $X$ _is_, I think it's fairly easy to put any story into one of three categories: "that can be done within a day or so", "that will probably fit in a (1-week) iteration" or "that would be a multi-iteration story that we need to break down somehow".


And then it is possible to have a discussion between client and developers about what value can be extracted from what work and thus, finally, which parts of which stories to include in _this_ iteration, which is all that matters.
