title=Mapping a Team on a Radar
saveAs=tech/team-radar


You may or may not know what a radar diagram is.  If you don't, I'm not going to explain it, but just &sp
&link 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_chart' 'send you off to Wikipedia'
.


In short, this is a way of measuring how one (or more) people or systems match up to certain "arbitrary" criteria.  You map the criteria on the axes, measure each person (or system) on each of the axes, choose a colour and join the dots.


There are, obviously, millions of applications for this.  It is particularly common in mapping personality types to observe the way that individuals react to certain stimuli.


In this context, I want to talk about using this technique to map how a team relates to a software project.  There is nothing formal or complicated going on here (I'm sure some management consultant can make it that way with questionnaires and whatever, but I believe in KISS - keep it simple, stupid).  Pick some arbitrary things that you think matter to a software project.  Here are some of mine:


* Feature Delivery
* Velocity
* Team Health
* Code Health
* Learning
* Building Capacity
* Stakeholder Relationships


Now, go through the team and, as a first pass, collect self-report data from each team member as to which of these really resonate with them: which of these, if ignored for a week, or a month, would keep them up at night.  I'll happily tell you mine: feature delivery, velocity and stakeholder relationships.


That is to say, by and large, we all agree that all these things _are_ important.  But some of them really trigger us when they are neglected, while others (particularly learning and building capacity for me) are things we are only incidentally aware of.  If there are other axes on your list that _aren't even on mine_, that tells you how important they are to me.


Now, for each team member, plot their self-report data on the radar diagram.  And see what you get.  In general, you will see a lot of overlap, but you will generally see some holes as well: does your team have anyone who _really_ cares about stakeholder relationships?  Anyone who _really_ cares about code health?  If you don't, you either need to go out and find someone who does (this is definitely feedback into hiring whether internal or external), or you all need to be vigilant that that area is not being neglected.


Now, I want to be really clear about one thing here: what we are talking about is _sensing_, not _doing_.  Just because you are about code health (and I don't) does NOT give me the right to check in crap and require you to fix it.  On the contrary, it gives you the "authority" to call me out when I am checking in crap and to work with me to improve my process.  Exactly how this works depends on your process, but having you pair with me for a week should at least help your values to rub off on me.  Even just the knowledge that my code is going to have to pass your review is probably enough.


That obviously cuts both ways: if you don't want to engage with the customer, that's your preference, but the team loses out if you don't.  I (or someone else like me) needs to work with you, _literally with_ you, to help you to improve your skills at working with the customer and finding out that they are a human, just like you.  I know; I've done it with all kinds of introverts.  Telling you to do it won't get you there; working _with_ you is what's required.


It's called teamwork.
